ROUND AL v2 — the button system, redone

His note on v1: "nhìn vẫn k đều … gợi ý bằng hình ảnh thay vì text, có thể k cần tất cả chung 1 dòng. Chủ yếu là nhìn thuận mắt." He is right, and the cause is visible: v1 forced every row onto an equal-share grid, so a one-word button and a two-word button got the same width — the text either squeezes or floats in dead space. Fixed below two ways: glyphs instead of words, and content-sized chips that wrap instead of a divided grid.

the tell: dead space vs squeezed text
In a divided grid the column width is set by the LONGEST cell, so every shorter cell carries padding it does not need, and the longest cell has no room to breathe. At 4 columns the field is ~253px, so each column is ~60px — "very fast" needs ~54px of text plus 8px of padding and lands flush against its borders. He read that as uneven. It is.

v2 — content-sized chips that WRAP (nothing stretched, nothing squeezed)

Every control is a chip whose width follows its own content, with a pill shape for word rows and a square for glyph rows. Rows wrap instead of dividing, so a 2-word option never has to fit a fixed column — and the rows do not all have to be the same length, which he explicitly allowed ("có thể k cần tất cả chung 1 dòng").

What changed from v1. v1 divided the row into equal columns, so the button width was decided by the longest word — that is the "không đều". v2 lets each chip take the width its own label needs and wrap the rest to the next line: nothing is stretched, nothing is squeezed, and a row may be as short or as long as its content.

Glyphs where a glyph can carry the meaning (colour, arrow ends, line style, curvature) — those rows are squares, so they are even automatically. Words only where there is no honest glyph (the flow modes, the speed words), and those are pills.

One height everywhere: 29px. That is the single number v1 got wrong, together with the grid.
